ILM is 'ilm — knowledge. It's also what we manage.

Islamic Learning Management. Seeking knowledge is an obligation on every Muslim; keeping track of it shouldn't be a burden on every school.

Built inside working schools, not around them.

Most school software treats the parts that matter most as an afterthought. A specialized program like Hifz becomes a spreadsheet on the side. Roll call happens on paper. Parents hear about a problem at the end of term, when it's too late to help.

ILM started the other way around: inside working schools, one workflow at a time. Roll call had to take under a minute or teachers wouldn't do it. For schools running a Hifz program, the Qari's revision rounds had to be first-class, not an afterthought. A parent's daily view had to show only what needed action — because a flooded parent stops reading.

Today ILM runs a complete school day end to end — every class, every teacher, every family — in the school it was built in. Every feature still earns its place the same way: a real principal's question, a real teacher's evening, a real parent's worry.

What we believe

Context beats ranking Numbers without context punish the wrong people. A blank row means "help this teacher start," not "this teacher is failing." Our own dashboards say so in their footers.
Adoption before analytics Software that isn't used measures nothing. Every workflow is designed to fit inside a real school day first — the insight is a byproduct of honest daily use.
Dignity in data A teacher never sees a ranked table of their peers. Comparison belongs only to those accountable for the whole — and even they see fairness windows on new accounts.
Hifz done right (optional) Schools that offer Qur'an memorization shouldn't run it from a side spreadsheet. Sabaq, sabqi, and manzil get the same engineering care as grades and attendance.
